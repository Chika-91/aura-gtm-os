## Style observations (INFERRED, from the pages above)

- INFERRED: confident, benefit-first, mission-driven tone; short sentences; abstract nouns such as "protection" and "response" more than concrete scenarios on the main pages.
- INFERRED: the Kenya homepage is a template shared with other regions. The meta description refers to South Africa, and customer stories listed are South Africa, UK and US.
- INFERRED: Kenyan press voice is more concrete (delivery riders, motorists, Nairobi roads, ambulance response times) than AURA's own site copy.
- Punctuation habit: AURA's site copy uses em dashes and an irregular spaced en dash in some lines. Not found whether any style guide exists. Our own rule is no em dashes in copy.

## Compliance and caution language AURA uses

- Terms say response availability depends on responders in an area and response times are not guaranteed (reported by the Standard). Any copy that promises a time should be checked against this. [src: https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/health/tech-innovation/article/2001558173/tech-driven-emergency-response-gains-ground-as-platform-targets-gaps-in-public-system | 2026-11-13]
